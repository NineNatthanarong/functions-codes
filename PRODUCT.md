# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students, teachers, and office workers in Thailand first, English second. They open a tool in the middle of a real task: crop a photo, convert a PDF, format JSON, generate a QR. They are on a laptop at a desk or a phone on the commute. They do not want an account, an ad, or a file uploaded to someone else's server.

## Product Purpose

functions.codes is a private tower of client-side tools. The job on any screen is to find the right room fast and finish the work in the browser. Success is: the file never left the device, the UI did not get in the way, and the person would open the same room again tomorrow.

The homepage is the lobby — atmosphere and wayfinding. Each tool page is a lit office: calm, dense enough to work, nothing theatrical blocking the task.

## Brand Personality

Composed, private, pixel-architectural.

Voice is dry hospitality: a concierge who does not make jokes, does not upsell, and does not panic. Thai is native, not translated-after. English is plain. No mascot. No hype. Pixel is the building; words stay human-sized and sharp.

Emotional goal: the relief of a quiet high-rise after hours — lights on, city outside, documents that stay in the building.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS tool directories (TinyWow / iLovePDF clutter, identical icon-title-description card grids)
- WeWork millennial pastel plants and “community” chrome
- Cyberpunk neon Tokyo / terminal-hacker skins for everyday tools
- Cream / sand / beige “warm paper” AI defaults
- Glassmorphism dashboards, gradient text, side-stripe callouts
- Bounce and elastic motion, orchestrated page-load choreography on tool pages
- Cute receptionist copy that talks over the task

## Design Principles

1. **Files never leave the building.** Privacy is architecture, not a badge farm. The metaphor should make the policy obvious.
2. **The lobby is theatrical; the office is quiet.** Spectacle lives on the homepage. Inside a tool, chrome gets out of the way of the file.
3. **One gimmick, skippable.** The elevator is the signature. It must never block reaching a tool. Reduced motion skips the ride.
4. **Motion describes space.** Doors, floors, lamps — not decoration. 150–250ms on product chrome; the ride may take a beat longer because it is the gimmick.
5. **Thai-first hospitality.** Warm, dry, never cute. Contrast, 44px targets, and reduced-motion alternatives are part of the building code.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor. Body and placeholder text ≥ 4.5:1. Focus visible, not a color-only change. Touch targets ≥ 44px on mobile. `prefers-reduced-motion` is mandatory: elevator becomes an instant floor change; skyline holds still; no door choreography. Thai and English must both wrap without overflow at mobile widths. No sound unless the user opts in (default: silent).
